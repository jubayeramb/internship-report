\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chapter-5}

\section{Summary}

\par Campus settings are controlled environments. Production settings are volatile environments. The difference became crystal clear during my eight-week at WeCycle. The point was not to simply observe and note the differences but to actually contribute to the system with your codes and understand the implications in a volatile production setting, where the effects of your codes could actually reach the end user almost instantly, whether it was the desired result or the opposite.

\par Firstly, the WeCycle Dashboard Application (WCAPP) was a crash course in complexity. While the syntax of React.js is simple in theory. Building a scalable app with resilience to regression is far from it. I learned to always favor understanding over brilliance.

\section{The Real Challenges}

\par \textbf{Challenges Faced:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Codebase Complexity:} At first, the number of files was intimidating. To understand a codebase that existed before the project, one had to build a mental diagram of component dependencies in addition to reviewing code.
    \item \textbf{Migration Friction:} Migrating between Ant Design and rolling out our own UI components is not some find-and-replace process and involved reworking core logic with existing workflows not being broken in any way.
    \item \textbf{Remote Communication:}
    Remote working has eliminated the privilege of shoulder-tapping. I had to learn to craft appropriate and contextually correct questions to unblock myself.
    \item \textbf{Agile Velocity:} it was a pace that never slowed. I discovered that delivering the code on time often matters more than delivering flawless code.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What I Actually Did (Scope)}

\par \textbf{Scope of Work:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Frontend Development:} Developing type-safe React components and connecting APIs.
    \item \textbf{System Architecture:} Analysis of file structures to preserve design patterns of modularity.
    \item \textbf{Responsive Implementation:} Handling complex layout shift issues on different device viewports.
    \item \textbf{Task Management:} Managing sprint velocity and tracking progress using Jira.
    \item \textbf{Version Control:} Feature branch management, merge conflict resolution in a team setting.
\end{itemize}

\section{Problems I Solved}

\par I didn't just learn. I shipped.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Migration:} Migrated 10+ pages to the new UI. The application is faster now.
    \item \textbf{Bugs:} Resolved the password reset (critical) issue.
    \item \textbf{New Pages:} Designed Job Details and Blog pages completely.
    \item \textbf{Tablets:} Squashed numerous iPad-specific bugs in layout.
\end{itemize}

\par My supervisor didn’t hold my hand. He told me what I needed to do and let me figure it out. Sometimes it was frustrating, but he pushed me to learn, really learn, about debugging instead of simply solving the problem for me.

\section{The Verdict (Overall Achievement)}

\par \textbf{Overall Value:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Competence:} I began applying my knowledge from the exercises to develop deployable software solutions.
    \item \textbf{Output:} Successfully addressed 70+ Jira tickets. Directly impacted product roadmap.
    \item \textbf{Discipline:} Most certainly, the discipline that the work-from-home experience taught me was the need for self-motivation.
    \item \textbf{Insight:} I learned that startups move at high speed and with high stakes.
\end{itemize}

 \par This internship was not just something I had to do, but it is where I changed. I went in knowing some react, and I came out knowing how to build products. I learned how to exist within such a quick environment and produce something of value. I no longer just study Computer Science, but I am now a software engineer ready to enter the world.
